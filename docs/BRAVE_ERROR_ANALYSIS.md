# Brave evidence-v2 error analysis — 2026-11-01

This audit concerns the benchmark rerun completed on 2026-10-31.

The evidence-v2 run answered more questions, but its acceptance checks did not reliably distinguish an independently established answer from an unverified factual guess. The gain in coverage exposed errors that the previous citation policy had suppressed. Some useful retrieved evidence was also discarded before revision. These findings come from an offline audit of the two completed 50-question development runs, their cached sources, candidate histories and existing Astra grades. No additional provider calls were made for this analysis.

Eighteen previous abstentions became answers: seven correct and eleven incorrect. Two previously correct answers became abstentions, leaving a net coverage increase of sixteen. Incorrect nonempty answers increased by ten: eleven former abstentions plus one former correct answer, minus two corrected answers. Accuracy among nonempty answers fell from 16/23 (69.6%) to 22/39 (56.4%), while overall accuracy increased from 16/50 to 22/50.

All thirteen questions previously forced to abstain by the citation gate received answers in the new run: seven correct and six incorrect. Of the other fourteen previous abstentions, five became incorrect answers and nine remained abstentions. These are paired outcome counts, not a controlled counterfactual: prompts, model samples, retrieval and final selection also changed.

The main findings are:

1. **The replacement gate has a factual-verification gap.** `source_claims` covers only facts the model says it used from supplied passages. Both independent derivations and answers recalled from model knowledge may have empty lists. In this J23 configuration, `adaptive` is false, so the separate constraint check is skipped. A nonempty answer with no declared claims or citations therefore passes `assess()` without a Jev support judgment. All seventeen incorrect final answers followed that path. Nine retained explicit gaps, including seven of the eleven former abstentions that became incorrect. See [candidate assessment](../src/jev_solver/controller/engine.py), [claim coverage and citation verification](../src/jev_solver/judgments/pipeline.py), and [the candidate prompt](../src/jev_solver/providers/backbone.py).

2. **The preservation instruction retained guesses as well as sound solutions.** Sixteen of the seventeen incorrect final answers were textually identical to that run's initial answer. The remaining answer added explanation while retaining the same incorrect selection. Thus these errors were already present before research; the new workflow failed to correct or withhold them. Seven newly incorrect items underwent research and explicitly acknowledged missing verification; four underwent no search. Several queries embedded the initial guessed answer, while others searched a generic sentence about missing verification. The latter behavior comes from falling back directly to `candidate.gaps` when no queries are supplied. Six of these seven researched items used only one of the two allowed search requests; a second neutral query was not generated after inconclusive evidence.

3. **The final selector provided little additional abstention.** There were forty calls to the Sol selector with a nonempty eligible pool; thirty-nine returned an answer, including all seventeen incorrect answers. Only one chose `none-supported`. Ten of the eleven final abstentions had no eligible candidate, so no selector call occurred. The selector receives the same model's proposals and verification summaries, together with `accepted: true` assessments whose empty failure lists can mean checks were never applicable or executed. Its instruction favors preserving an answer absent a refutation. The acceptance rate is observed; prompt anchoring and correlated model errors are explanations consistent with the traces, not isolated causal estimates. See [selection](../src/jev_solver/candidates/selection.py).

4. **Relevance is judged against the query without the original question.** A cached primary-source article contained the benchmark's expected answer to an image-based factual question. The answering passage reached Jev's shortlist but received normalized relevance 0.357 and was discarded by the 0.667 threshold. A different passage matching the solver's guessed feature received 0.767 and survived. Jev saw the answer-biased query, passage, URL and title, but not the original question. This is a directly traced loss of available evidence inside the pipeline. The seven researched abstention-to-error items received two empty packets and five packets that did not establish their final answers; one of those five supported only a different part of a multipart answer. The private report contains the exact source, passage IDs and cached judgments.

5. **Research can also be skipped by confident mistakes.** If an initial candidate supplies no gaps, queries or code, the controller proceeds to final selection. Eight of the seventeen incorrect answers followed this path, including four former abstentions and the sole correct-to-incorrect transition. The latter changed its image identification in the initial response and received no research in the new run. Fresh sampling and prompt changes therefore contribute to the comparison. Wrong answers had mean reported confidence 84.9%; seven were at least 90% confident. A confidence cutoff alone is not a demonstrated fix.

Jev made 497 relevance judgments and six citation-support judgments in the new run. It was not asked to verify the seventeen selected incorrect answers. This distinction matters when interpreting a J23 result as evidence about Jev's ability to assess answer correctness.

There is a positive example: a retrieved research passage corrected an initial answer on a combinatorial construction, the revised answer declared and cited the dependency, Jev checked support, and final selection retained the correction. The previous run had also produced the corrected answer but then suppressed it for missing citations. Restoring the blanket citation gate would lose useful independently derived answers and this kind of repair.

The next changes should address the observed failure paths:

- Distinguish checked derivations, evidence-supported facts and unverified prior knowledge. Assess critical factual dependencies independently of the model's optional use of retrieved sources; represent checks that were not performed separately from passed checks. Unresolved necessary facts should trigger further verification or abstention under the chosen coverage policy.
- Give relevance judgments the original question and a neutral statement of the missing fact. Preserve relevant contradictory evidence. Replace raw gap sentences and answer-assuming queries with focused queries; plan additional verification queries and reserve fetch capacity within the existing caps when the first packet does not resolve the gap.
- Require the selector to identify why a candidate is established and whether unresolved gaps affect its answer. Evaluate this independently on saved candidates; do not tune a confidence threshold on these same fifty reference labels.
- Compare the corrected J23 workflow with matched B2 and, where feasible, shared initial candidates and cached sources. Changing to Perplexity alone would leave the acceptance and relevance-context defects in place. A provider comparison needs an explicitly frozen pipeline.

The grades remain those already reported. Some failures concern disagreement with an expected formulation or unstated assumptions, rather than an obviously fabricated fact; two newly incorrect items have this character. One nominal wrong-to-correct gain is sensitive to asymptotic notation and the grader's interpretation. These should be reviewed separately without silently changing the reported counts. This small, repeatedly inspected development split cannot establish a reliable generalization gain.
